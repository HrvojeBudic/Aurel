# P1.4 — Research Alignment Notes

Working notes on how the P1.4 identity and autonomy design aligns with the
broader safety intent of the runtime. These are rationale, not law; the law is
in the scope contract and the trust constitution.

## Why these separations

The recurring failure mode in agent systems is *category confusion*: letting
identity, persona, or mode leak into permission. P1.4 draws the lines sharply so
the leak cannot happen:

* **Identity is not policy** keeps self-description from becoming self-permission.
* **Persona is not authority** keeps stance from becoming scope.
* **Mode is not permission** keeps framing (including heretic framing) from
  becoming execution rights.
* **Operator remains final authority** and **Aurel cannot self-escalate** together
  ensure the human stays the root of trust under every level.

## Evidence over labels

The runtime's alignment posture is empirical: **capability claims require
evidence**. A green test, a passing linter, or a confident docstring is a claim
to be falsified by execution, not a fact. This is why capabilities are attested
(sandbox isolation is functionally probed; the trace is externally anchored)
rather than declared.

## Autonomy as a manual spectrum

Autonomy is a chosen level, not an emergent property. The governance scale spans
`ABSOLUTE GOVERNED` to `HERETIC`; the measured autonomy score informs, but never
performs, a level change. Heretic mode is cognitive freedom, not uncontrolled
execution — a deliberately bounded freedom that keeps the constitutional floor.

## Forward alignment hooks

The identity work sets up evaluation (**P1.5**), policy (**P1.6**), path
governance (**P1.7**), delegation boundaries (**P1.8**), and the integration
tail (**P1.9**). Each later phase inherits the invariant that trust is earned and
audited, and that the operator holds the final authority.
